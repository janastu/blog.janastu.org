# AnthillHacks 2026: ReVoice - Recognizing Voices, Celebrating Stories
**Date**: December 23, 2026 to January 2, 2027 

**Place**: Janastu Base Camp, Halekote, Tumkuru - 572140

## ReVoice 

Mainstream narratives overlook the many perspectives that make up our world. To create a world that works for all, everyone's experiences, knowledge, and perspectives must be valued. ReVoice is about recognizing these excluded voices, acknowledging their value, and creating spaces where they can be heard.



## Introduction 


Janastu has been working with rural and marginalized communities to create decentralized alternatives in technology, knowledge archiving, and livelihoods. Through community-owned wireless mesh networks, community storytelling and knowledge archiving platforms, and community-led crafts, Janastu seeks to ensure that people have the means to shape their own futures and preserve their knowledge and cultures.


Over the years, AnthillHacks has brought together communities, technologists, artists, environmentalists, researchers, and thinkers to explore alternative ways of living, creating, and building together. This year, AnthillHacks takes up the theme ***ReVoice***. ReVoice invites us to question whose voices are heard. It asks us to listen more carefully, recognize different ways of knowing, and create spaces and platforms for these voices to emerge. 


## Threads of ReVoice 


We have identified four interconnected threads of *ReVoice* that we intend to explore at Anthillhacks 2026.


**1. Celebrating Knowledge and Stories**

Rural landscapes hold layers of practical and historical knowledge that are undocumented and unrecognized. Some folks carry a wealth of knowledge about the hills and forests, ancient temples, and historical sites. Others possess intimate familiarity with local ecosystems and the ways in which their communities have lived with their natural surroundings. Older people have knowledge of making traditional tools and utilities from local materials. 


Communities also have distinctive stories, about both the mundane and the unusual, often carrying memories of places, people, and traditions. These stories may offer unique ways of looking at the world. 


At AnthillHacks 2026, we would like to recognize and celebrate these knowledge-holders and storytellers. The event will explore ways of documenting, preserving and sharing their knowledge and stories through community archiving, audio and video recordings, written documentation in Kannada, community radio, and other forms. 



**2. Recognizing Women’s Voices and Lived Experiences**

Women in rural communities often face multiple forms of marginalization. Their voices and perspectives are overlooked not only because they belong to rural communities, but also because of their gender. Even when they speak, their experiences may be dismissed, normalized, or silenced.


ReVoice is also about creating safe spaces and platforms where rural women can voice their views and lived experiences. When women hear others speak about experiences similar to their own, it can help them recognize that they are not alone, build solidarity, challenge prevailing attitudes about women, and inspire collective action.


We are working with PLASMA, our platform for safe storytelling, to explore how women can record their stories safely in closed groups and decide what to share more widely. Features such as voice anonymization can help women speak about issues they might otherwise find difficult or unsafe to discuss openly. 


We would like to explore this area further at the event with experts working on women's rights, community-led storytelling, digital safety, psychosocial support, and collective action. We are interested in ideas and activities that can help us think about the relationship between safe storytelling, community solidarity, and social change.


**4. Technology, Ecology and the Commons**

The theme of ReVoice also impels us to think about the technologies, infrastructures, and environments that shape whose voices are heard and whose knowledge is accessible.


We would like to explore the possibilities of community-owned communication networks, including wireless mesh networks, and how they can support local knowledge sharing and community archives. We are also interested in emerging technologies such as small language models and their potential for working with local languages and knowledge systems. Another area we would like to explore is the relationship between technology and ecology. 

We hope for the event to bring together people working in technology and ecology to explore our relationship with local landscapes, ecosystems, and the natural commons.


## Shaping AnthillHacks Together
These are the broad areas we would like to explore at AnthillHacks 2026. We hope to learn from and collaborate with people working in community archiving, oral histories, technology, women’s rights, digital safety, community psychology, ecology, art and other fields.
