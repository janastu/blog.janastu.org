# AnthillHacks 2026: ReVoice - Recognizing Voices, Celebrating Stories
**Date**: December 23, 2026 to January 2, 2027 

**Place**: Janastu Base Camp, Halekote, Tumkuru - 572140

## ReVoice 

Mainstream narratives overlook the many perspectives that make up our world. But to create a world that works for all, everyone's experiences, knowledge, and perspectives must be valued. ReVoice is about recognizing these excluded voices, acknowledging their value, and creating spaces where they can be heard.



## Introduction 


Janastu has been working with rural and marginalized communities to create decentralized alternatives in technology, knowledge archiving, and livelihoods. Through community-owned wireless mesh networks, community storytelling and knowledge archiving platforms, and community-led crafts, Janastu seeks to ensure that people have the means to shape their own futures and preserve their knowledge and cultures.


Over the years, AnthillHacks has brought together communities, technologists, artists, environmentalists, researchers, and thinkers to explore alternative ways of living, creating, and building together. This year, AnthillHacks takes up the theme ***ReVoice***. ReVoice invites us to question whose voices are heard. It asks us to listen more carefully, recognize different ways of knowing, and create spaces and platforms for these voices to emerge. 


## Threads of ReVoice 


We have identified four interconnected threads of *ReVoice* that we intend to explore at Anthillhacks 2026.


**1. Recognising Knowledge**

Rural landscapes hold layers of cultural and historical knowledge that are undocumented and unrecognized. Rural people’s understanding of the land, nature, agriculture, materials, and everyday life comes from lived experience. 


Some carry a wealth of knowledge about the hills and forests, ancient temples, and historical sites. Others possess intimate familiarity with local ecosystems and the ways in which their communities have lived with their natural surroundings. Older people have knowledge of making traditional tools and utilities from local materials. These skills and practices represent generations of experimentation, adaptation, and learning. 


At AnthillHacks 2026, we would like to explore ways of documenting and preserving this knowledge through community archives, local-language documentation, audio and video recordings, and other creative forms. 


**2. Celebrating Stories and Oral Histories**

Every community has people with stories to tell. Some are about everyday life or unusual experiences, and others carry memories of places, people, and traditions. Often, they offer unique ways of looking at the world. 


ReVoice is about recognizing such storytellers and creating opportunities for their stories to emerge. It is about listening without prejudice and valuing different forms of expression. 
We would like to make space for people to share and document these stories in different ways during AnthillHacks 2026. This could include oral history recordings, audio and video storytelling, conversations, and other creative forms of expression. We are interested in exploring ways to preserve these stories without losing the context in which they are told or the voices and perspectives of the people telling them.


**3. Recognizing Women’s Voices**

Women in rural communities often face multiple forms of marginalization. Their voices and perspectives are overlooked not only because they belong to rural communities, but also because of their gender. Even when they speak, their experiences may be dismissed, normalized, or silenced.


ReVoice is also about creating safe spaces and platforms where rural women can voice their views and lived experiences. When women hear others speak about experiences similar to their own, it can help them recognize that they are not alone, build solidarity, challenge prevailing ideas and attitudes about women, and inspire collective action.


We are working with PLASMA, our safe storytelling platform, to explore how women can record their stories safely in closed groups and decide what to share more widely. Features such as voice anonymization can help women speak about issues they might otherwise find difficult or unsafe to discuss openly. 


At AnthillHacks 2026, we would like to explore this area further with experts working on women's rights, community-led storytelling, digital safety, psychosocial support, and collective action. We are interested in ideas and activities that can help us think about the relationship between safe storytelling, community solidarity, and social change.



**4. Technology, Ecology and the Commons**

ReVoice also invites us to think about the technologies, infrastructures, and environments that shape whose voices are heard and whose knowledge is accessible.


We would like to explore the possibilities of community-owned communication networks, including wireless mesh networks, and how they can support local communication, knowledge sharing, and community archives. We are also interested in emerging technologies such as small language models and their potential for working with local languages and knowledge systems. Can these technologies make information and knowledge more accessible to communities? What are the possibilities and limitations? How do we ensure that communities retain control over their knowledge and how it is used?


Another area we would like to explore is the relationship between technology and ecology. What can we learn from the intersection of blockchain, crypto technologies, and environmental sustainability? We would like to bring together people working in ecology and environmental conservation to explore our relationship with local landscapes, ecosystems, and the natural commons.


## Shaping AnthillHacks Together
These are the broad areas we would like to explore at AnthillHacks 2026, and we hope to learn from and collaborate with people working in community archiving, oral histories, technology, women’s rights, digital safety, community psychology, ecology and related fields.
